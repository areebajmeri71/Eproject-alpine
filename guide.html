What Is Mountaineering?

Mountaineering, also called mountain climbing, is the sport of reaching (or trying to reach) high points in mountain areas, mostly for the joy of the climb itself. People sometimes use the word for an easy walk up a small hill, but real mountaineering happens where the ground and the weather are dangerous enough that you need experience to stay safe.

Unlike most sports, mountaineering has no single set of official rules. Climbers follow many different techniques and ideas about how a mountain should be climbed. What they all share is careful planning, the right skills and a lot of respect for the mountain. For someone without training, it can be a very dangerous hobby.

On this page we have collected the basics: its history, the main types, the techniques climbers use, how they shelter, the hazards, famous records, where to find us and some success stories.

History of Mountaineering

Modern mountaineering began in 1786, when Jacques Balmat and Michel-Gabriel Paccard reached the top of Mont Blanc in the Alps. The years from 1854 to 1865 are called the "Golden Age of Alpinism", when most big Alpine peaks were climbed for the first time, ending with the Matterhorn in 1865.

  • 1857: The Alpine Club, the world's first mountaineering club, is started in London.
  • 1950: Annapurna becomes the first 8,000 m peak to be climbed.
  • 1953: Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay reach the top of Everest.
  • 1954: An Italian team makes the first ascent of K2 in Pakistan.
  • 1986: Reinhold Messner becomes the first person to climb all fourteen 8,000 m peaks.

Types and Styles

There are many ways to climb a mountain. The main types are:

  • Trekking / hillwalking: long walks on mountain trails, no special climbing gear.
  • Rock climbing: climbing steep rock with hands and feet, protected by a rope.
  • Ice climbing: climbing frozen waterfalls and ice using ice axes and crampons.
  • Alpine climbing: mixed rock, snow and ice on high peaks, usually in one push.
  • Ski mountaineering: climbing up on skis and skiing back down.

Big climbs are done in two styles: expedition style, with fixed ropes and a chain of camps, or the faster and lighter alpine style, where climbers carry everything with them.

Climbing Techniques

Every climber learns a set of basic skills before going high:

  • Rope team: climbers tie into one rope so the others can hold a fall.
  • Belaying: one person controls the rope to catch the climber if they slip.
  • Abseiling (rappelling): sliding down a fixed rope in a controlled way.
  • Crampon technique: walking safely on hard snow and ice.
  • Self-arrest: stopping a slide on snow by digging in the ice axe.
  • Acclimatisation: "climb high, sleep low", so the body gets used to thin air.

Sheltering in the Mountains

On long climbs, a good shelter can save your life. Climbers use:

  • Tents: strong four-season tents that stand up to wind and snow.
  • Base camp: the main camp at the foot of the mountain, with food and supplies.
  • Mountain huts: stone or wooden huts on popular routes, like in the Alps.
  • Bivouac: a simple night out with a bivy bag, often on a small ledge.
  • Snow caves and igloos: dug or built from snow in an emergency; they stay warmer than a tent.

Mountain Hazards

The mountains can be dangerous. The biggest risks are:

  • Avalanches: large amounts of snow suddenly sliding down a slope.
  • Crevasses: deep cracks in a glacier, often hidden under snow.
  • Rockfall and icefall: loose rocks or ice blocks falling from above.
  • Altitude sickness: headache, sickness and worse, caused by thin air up high.
  • Cold: frostbite and hypothermia in low temperatures and strong wind.
  • Bad weather: storms and whiteouts that make it impossible to see the way.

Success Stories

Famous expeditions where careful planning and teamwork got climbers to the top.

Everest, 1953

A British expedition of more than 400 people, porters included, set up a chain of camps so that Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay could make the first ascent.

K2, 1954

An Italian expedition with help from Pakistani high-altitude porters made the first ascent of the "Savage Mountain" in the Karakoram.

Winter K2, 2021

Ten Nepali climbers joined forces and reached the top together on 16 January 2021, the first time anyone climbed K2 in winter.

Samina Baig, 2013

With a small team, Samina Baig became the first Pakistani woman to stand on top of Mount Everest.
